Bio

I am an astronomical researcher focusing on galaxy evolution, with interests in dust-obscured galaxies and the formation history of galaxy clusters. I am currently a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Ljubljana Faculty of Mathematics and Physics.

My work utilizes observations primarily from the James Webb Space Telescope with supporting data from the Hubble Space Telescope and ground-based observatories

Research summary

Theme 1: Dust-obscured Galaxies

Most of the light emitted by newly formed stars over the history of the universe is absorbed and re-emitted by dust. In order to provide an accurate accounting of the star formation history of the universe, properly we need to understand processes of dust attenuation and emission in detail. My research has focused on determining the number and properties of dusty star-forming galaxies pushing to the highest redshifts.

Theme 2: Formation History of Galaxy Clusters

Galaxy clusters serve as one of the best test beds for studying the related growth of structure from both dark and luminous matter. By studying the relative spatial distribution of intracluster light, globular clusters, and dark matter in galaxy clusters, I hope to understand how they for. Possibly, we can uncover a way to use these luminous components as direct tracers of the underlying dark matter.
